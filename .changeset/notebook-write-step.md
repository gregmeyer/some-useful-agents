A meeting notebook's last step writes the talking points instead of asking you to pick articles.

A new step kind, `write`: sua's writer (`notebook-writer`) reads what the notebook is for, who it's about and the themes it found, and writes 3–5 talking points, each a point to raise, why it matters to them, a question, and the themes it rests on (their quotes and sources are shown as they are, never rewritten), plus questions to ask and things to watch for. The briefing's agenda and the brief show them. The drafter and sua end meeting notebooks with it instead of a decide step.
